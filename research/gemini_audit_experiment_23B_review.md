# Independent review of Gemini's audit of Experiment 23B

Date: 2026-11-26  
Status: Partial acceptance; individual per-block-length comparisons remain useful, but the reported global p-value must be withdrawn and several claims in Gemini's audit need qualification.

## Executive summary

Gemini correctly identified that subtracting the same analytic covariance term from the observed statistic and every simulated statistic makes that term cancel from the reported centered z-scores and tail probabilities. This means the reported Monte Carlo comparisons do not validate the analytic covariance calculation. It does not, by itself, make the per-b Monte Carlo comparisons invalid: they still compare the observed cross-product statistic with the simulated distribution generated by the stated model.

Gemini is also right that the nine-dimensional maximum test does not preserve the dependence structure of the nine observed statistics. Separate simulations for b=10,30,210 were combined by replicate index, but those independent simulation streams do not reproduce the dependence induced by the common observed prime sequence and nested block partitions. The value 0.9652 should therefore be withdrawn as a calibrated global p-value. It is not established that it is necessarily conservative; that depends on the joint dependence structure.

The three b-specific null models do have different conditioning rules. However, this does not make a joint null mathematically impossible. One can define a coherent coupling across b by sharing compatible random residues and thinning uniforms where appropriate, or avoid a joint model and use a dependence-robust multiplicity correction such as Bonferroni. The original implementation did neither.

## 1. Mathematical derivation

**Accepted.** For an eligible n, the survival probability over randomized residues is
\[
\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\p\nmid b}}(1-1/p).
\]
Since
\[
\Theta_z=\prod_{p\mid b}(1-1/p)
\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\p\nmid b}}(1-1/p)
=\frac{\phi(b)}b
\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\p\nmid b}}(1-1/p),
\]
this survival probability is \((b/\phi(b))\Theta_z\). Multiplication by the thinning probability \(1/(\Theta_z\log n)\) gives the marginal \(Q/\log n\), where \(Q=b/\phi(b)\).

For two eligible integers n,m with d=m-n, the joint survival probability is
\[
\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\p\nmid b}}
(1-\nu_p(d)/p),
\]
where \(\nu_p(d)=1\) if p divides d and 2 otherwise. Independent thinning gives the joint retained probability equal to this product divided by \(\Theta_z^2\log n\log m\). This is exactly the expression used by the code. The code's sing[d] stores the unnormalized finite local product, not the normalized singular series. Its use as sing[d]/(theta*theta*log(n)*log(m)) is correct.

## 2. Alignment and indexing

**Accepted with a defensive-code caveat.** For this interval, LO=100171 is 1 modulo 210 and hence 1 modulo each b. The active offsets gcd(1+r,b)==1 therefore match the eligible integers in every block. The residue sieve's first index correctly solves LO+j congruent to a modulo p. The maximum possible difference for k≤3 is 4b−1; because both eligible integers are odd, d is even. For b=210, d≤839 and in fact d≤838, so no prime greater than 613 can divide d.

The source should assert (LO-1)%b==0 and should derive active offsets from LO+r, rather than hardcoding the phase 1 assumption.

## 3. Cutoff and relationship to full Hardy–Littlewood

**Accepted with caveats.** The finite cutoff condition is internally coherent. The fixed z=613 model has exact, explicitly defined finite probabilities over the interval, and q_n≤1 throughout.

It is not the infinite Hardy–Littlewood singular-series model. Since all relevant d are even and d≤838, no prime p>613 divides d. The omitted normalized local factors for p>613 are therefore the common product
\[
E_{613}=\prod_{p>613}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right)<1.
\]
Thus the infinite-product pair factor is the finite-cutoff factor multiplied by E_613. The finite-cutoff pair joint probabilities are slightly larger than their infinite-product counterparts. Gemini's qualitative warning is correct. Its quoted relative percentages for particular b,k should be treated as approximate until recomputed from the exact finite sums; they are not needed to validate the finite model.

The cutoff rule is inspired by the BFT random model but the b-conditioned fixed-zero residue choices are an adaptation. The experiment must not describe it as the original BFT model or as a test of the full Hardy–Littlewood conjecture.

## 4. Expected value and covariance subtraction

**Accepted.** Under the stated finite model, the block mean and covariance formulas imply E[T_bk]=0.

**Important qualification to Gemini's criticism.** The covariance term cancels algebraically from the reported standardized z-score and absolute-deviation p-value because the same deterministic K is subtracted from the observed statistic and every simulation:
\[
(T_{obs}-\overline T_{sim})/s_T
=(S_{obs}-\overline S_{sim})/s_S.
\]
This is a genuine diagnostic defect: the published p-values do not test whether the analytic covariance array is correct. A wrong covariance array would not change these p-values.

However, this cancellation does not automatically invalidate each per-b Monte Carlo comparison. Those comparisons still test whether the observed raw residual cross-product statistic is typical of the simulated raw cross-product distribution under the implemented model. The analytic covariance subtraction is redundant for that rank comparison. It is necessary only if the stated statistic is to be interpreted as a zero-mean model-centered statistic or used to validate the analytic covariance.

Recommended correction: calculate the observed statistic as the average raw residual cross-product, simulate the same raw statistic, and report (a) the exact analytic model covariance average separately, (b) the simulated mean and its Monte Carlo standard error, and (c) the observed-minus-analytic-covariance residual. Use a separate simulation batch for calibration if z-standardization is retained. Better still, use empirical Monte Carlo tail ranks on the raw statistic centered at the model's exact expectation.

## 5. Global maximum test

**Needs correction.** The observed statistics at b=10,30,210 are calculated from the same prime sequence, and the block partitions are nested. The null simulations were generated independently for each b, so the maxNull array does not reproduce the joint dependence of the observed vector.

The b-specific models also condition differently: for example, p=3 is randomized for b=10 but fixed at zero for b=30 and 210; p=7 is randomized for b=10 and 30 but fixed for b=210. A joint model is nevertheless possible if explicitly constructed. For example, draw shared residues for p=3 and p=7 where they are randomized in the relevant b models, impose zero where that b conditions on zero, share residues for primes not dividing any of the b values, and define a consistent coupling of thinning uniforms. The resulting marginal model for each b must be checked against its intended distribution.

Simpler alternative: report the nine marginal tests and apply Bonferroni (or Holm) correction, which does not require a joint coupling if the marginal p-values are valid. The smallest reported marginal p is 0.3307, so Bonferroni gives an adjusted p-value of 1.0. This supports the same no-rejection conclusion at conventional levels.

Do not claim the independent max calibration is necessarily conservative. Its direction is not established without the actual joint dependence structure.

## 6. Monte Carlo centering

The code estimates the mean and standard deviation from the same 5,000 simulations used to form the empirical tail. The mean-estimation uncertainty is approximately one over square-root 5,000, or 1.4% of one simulation SD. This is small but not zero.

Because the exact model expectation of the centered statistic is zero, a stronger diagnostic is to report whether the simulated mean is within its Monte Carlo standard error of zero. For exact finite-sample rank calibration, use a separately generated calibration sample for standardization and an independent evaluation sample for tail ranks, or simply use empirical tails of the raw statistic around its known theoretical expectation.

## 7. Data validation

A second, independent segmented sieve was run after the initial data generation. It returned 8,407 primes in [100171,200341), with first prime 100183 and last prime 200329. This independently confirms the prime list count and endpoints. Each of the three block-count datasets sums to 8,407.

The C++ experiment file itself does not assert these totals or print them. Add defensive checks for the prime count, block totals, alignment and thinning-probability bounds, or preserve a separate reproducible validation script.

## 8. Power claims

**Gemini overstates what can be inferred from its signal-to-noise table.** The reported standard deviation of T under the random-sieve model is not automatically the standard deviation under a zero-covariance alternative. Dividing an approximate model covariance by the null SD does not by itself establish the power of a test against zero covariance, nor justify a relative-uncertainty range. A proper power calculation requires a specified alternative distribution and simulation under that alternative.

It is reasonable to say that the b=210 estimates are noisier because there are only 477 blocks, and that the present experiment does not sharply resolve small differences for those statistics. But the numerical claims of 2.8 sigma, 0.9 sigma, 0.5 sigma and the claimed 15–40% relative uncertainty should not be adopted without a formal power analysis and exact covariance calculations.

The individual Monte Carlo p-values do support a narrower statement: none of the nine observed raw cross-product statistics was unusual relative to its corresponding b-specific simulation distribution. The smallest reported p-value was about 0.331. This is not proof of model truth or independence.

## 9. Held-out status and provenance

The interval is disjoint from the previously described Experiments 19–22 ranges. Experiment 23A was a finite-model implementation validation using synthetic simulations, not actual prime data from this interval. The 23B protocol was committed before the actual interval's prime-count analysis. The audit should nevertheless retain explicit provenance links and timestamps in the repository.

## 10. Required repository corrections

1. Withdraw global_p_plus_one=0.9652 as a calibrated global p-value. Replace with nine marginal results plus Bonferroni/Holm, or rerun under a coherent joint coupling.
2. Explain that subtracting analytic covariance cancels from the reported Monte Carlo z and p values; the existing p-values compare raw cross-products to the simulated raw-cross-product distribution.
3. Add a check of the simulated mean against its theoretical zero expectation, and report model covariance averages separately.
4. Add assertions for alignment and q_n≤1; include explicit data-total checks.
5. Remove or qualify the claimed signal-to-noise/power conclusions.
6. Retain the finite-cutoff model as a finite random-sieve baseline, not full Hardy–Littlewood.

## Final verdict

The one-point and pair-probability mathematics and the per-b simulation mechanism are correct for the specified finite conditioned model. The reported individual Monte Carlo comparisons are still informative as model-compatibility checks, but they do not independently validate the analytic covariance formula. The global maximum p-value is not properly calibrated. The result should be archived with that global p-value withdrawn and with the power claims narrowed.
